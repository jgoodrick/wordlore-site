Privacy Policy

Effective 17 November 2026

Wordlore collects no data. There is no account, no analytics, no advertising and no tracking. Your words stay on your devices and in your own iCloud.

Who this covers

This policy covers the Wordlore app for iPhone, iPad and Mac, made by Joseph Goodrick, an independent developer. Questions go to support@josephgoodrick.com.

What the developer receives

Nothing. Wordlore has no server and no sign-in. The developer cannot see your words, your notes, your photos, your recordings, where you were, or how you use the app.

Where your collection is kept

Everything you add (words, meanings, examples, notes, photos, recordings and, if you allow it, the place where you met a word) is stored on your device.

If you are signed in to iCloud, Wordlore syncs your collection between your own devices through your private iCloud database. That database belongs to your Apple Account. The developer has no access to it. Apple handles it under Apple's privacy policy.

Permissions the app may ask for

Each one is optional, asked for only when you use the feature that needs it, and can be changed at any time in the Settings app.

  • Camera: to scan a word from printed text, or to attach a photo to a word.
  • Microphone and speech recognition: to add a word by saying it, to practise aloud, or to record a pronunciation. Speech is recognised on your device where your language supports it. For other languages, Apple's speech recognition may process the audio, under Apple's privacy policy.
  • Location: to remember where you met a word. The place is saved with the word in your own collection and nowhere else. You can turn this off in the app: Settings, then More, then Remember places.
  • Translation: meanings are translated on your device with Apple's translation feature.

Features that use Apple Intelligence run on your device.

The one thing the app downloads

Wordlore downloads dictionary files for the languages you choose. They are public files hosted on GitHub. The app sends nothing about you with the request. As with any download, GitHub's servers see the IP address the request comes from; see GitHub's privacy statement.

Crash reports

Wordlore includes no third-party crash or analytics software. If you chose to share analytics with app developers in your device's settings, Apple may give the developer anonymous, aggregated crash and usage figures. They do not identify you.

Deleting your data

Delete a word in the app and it is removed from your devices and your iCloud. Deleting the app removes its data from that device. To remove the synced copy, open the Settings app, tap your name, then iCloud, then manage storage, and delete Wordlore's data.

Children

Wordlore collects no data from anyone, including children.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the new version will be posted on this page with a new effective date.
